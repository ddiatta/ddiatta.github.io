Research

My research lies at the intersection of development, public, and labor economics. I study how households and workers respond to economic shocks and policy interventions, with a focus on social protection, informality, food security, and labor markets.

Working Papers

Shock Interactions and Food Security in Malawi
With Nathan Jensen and Joanna Upton
Working Paper
This paper examines how different economic and environmental shocks interact to affect household food security in Malawi. Using repeated household survey data, we study whether exposure to multiple shocks produces effects that differ from those associated with individual shocks.
The Worker’s Side of Formalization: Health Insurance and Workers’ Employment Choices in Senegal
Dieynab Diatta
Working Paper
This paper studies how the expansion of health insurance for workers in Senegal affects employment choices and formalization. I examine whether the introduction of a semi-contributory health insurance scheme changed workers’ access to health insurance and their participation in formal employment.

The Vicious Cycle of Food Insecurity and Geopolitical Instability
With Christopher B. Barrett
Work in Progress
This project examines the relationship between food insecurity and geopolitical instability, with particular attention to the role of food prices and social unrest.
