I am a PhD student in Applied Economics and Management at the Charles H. Dyson School, Cornell University, where I am advised by Christopher B. Barrett. My research lies at the intersection of development, public, and labor economics. I study how households and workers respond to economic and social shocks—including conflict, sociopolitical instability, and food price and climate disruptions— and how public programs and institutions can be designed to help people manage these risks.

A central focus of my research is the design of social protection and other public programs in settings where formal institutions and labor markets are limited. I am interested in how program design affects access, participation, and economic decisions, particularly among workers and households in the informal economy. This includes questions about how programs can be structured to reach vulnerable populations, how eligibility and benefits shape incentives, and why potentially valuable programs are sometimes underutilized.

My current projects examine the links between conflict and food security, the role of labor informality in shaping access to social protection, and the drivers of health insurance take-up. Across these projects, I seek to understand how policy design and institutional context shape people's ability to respond to shocks and improve their economic security.

Before Cornell, I worked with the World Bank, IFPRI, and IFAD in Dakar and Rome, and conducted fieldwork across the Sahel.
